# 3 · Select what is worth quantifying

Quantification costs real effort and needs inputs that often do not exist.
Spending it evenly across the register is the standard failure, and quantifying
a Low risk is how a risk process becomes theatre.

## Quantify when the answer changes a decision

- **A contingency reserve has to be sized.** Sum EMV across the top band. This is
  the main legitimate use of EMV, and why EMV must be a real quantity.
- **Two options must be chosen between.** A decision tree, with the branch
  probabilities written where somebody can disagree with them.
- **A date or a cost must be committed to.** PERT, with the spread.
- **Effort must be directed.** Sensitivity — usually the highest-value method
  here, because most projects have two or three dominant uncertainties and a long
  tail that does not matter, while effort is typically spread evenly.

## Do not quantify when

- the ranking already answers the question — which is most of the time;
- the inputs would be invented. A number built from a guess is a guess with more
  decimal places and a great deal more authority;
- the risk is outside the top band.

## Check the input exists before choosing the method

EMV needs a real probability (0–1) and a real amount. If the team can supply the
amount but not the probability, say so and stay with the ranking rather than
converting a 5×5 rating into a fraction. That conversion is invented precision: a
"4" does not mean 0.8, and nothing in the scale says it does.
