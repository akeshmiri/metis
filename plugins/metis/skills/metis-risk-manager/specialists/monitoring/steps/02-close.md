# 2 · Close, and keep the lesson

## Close with a reason, always

One of four, recorded on the row:

| Reason | Means |
|---|---|
| **Occurred** | It happened. Record the actual impact against the predicted one |
| **No longer applicable** | The cause is gone — scope changed, the technology was replaced |
| **Mitigated to acceptable** | The residual sits inside appetite. Record the residual |
| **Accepted** | A decision, with an owner and a reserve. Not a drift |

A row that disappears without one of these takes its lesson with it, and the
register loses the only thing that lets it improve.

## Compare the forecast with what happened

For an occurred risk, put the actual impact next to the predicted one. This is
the single most valuable and most commonly skipped step in the whole lifecycle: a
process that never checks its forecasts against outcomes cannot calibrate, and
its numbers stay unfalsifiable no matter how many reviews it holds.

Where the register is consistently wrong in one direction — impacts always worse
than rated, probabilities always over-stated — that is a finding about the scale,
not about the individual risks. Fix the scale.

## For a model-derived risk, closure can be checked

This is the one case in the register where closure is not a judgement. The risk
was *behaviour nothing validates*; it closes when something validates it. Re-run
`risk_candidates` — if the candidate no longer appears, the gap is closed by
evidence.

Two cautions:

- **Coverage is not correctness** (C-11). The behaviour is now tested. Whether
  the test passes is a different figure, and closing on coverage while the test
  is red is exactly the conflation the rule exists to prevent.
- **A candidate can vanish because nobody looked.** Check `depth_consulted`
  before reading its absence as progress.

## Capture the lesson where it will be found

A lessons document nobody opens at the start of the next project is a lesson
lost. The durable form is a **checklist entry** or an **RBS category** — the
prompts the next identification step actually walks. A lesson that changes what
gets asked next time is kept; a lesson in a report is filed.
