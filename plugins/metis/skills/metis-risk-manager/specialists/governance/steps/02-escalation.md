# 2 · Escalation, and the trail it leaves

## What escalation is

Handing a risk to whoever holds the authority to respond, because the response
needed is beyond this project's remit. It is a **strategy**, not a failure, and
it appears in both the threat and the opportunity sets.

## What escalation is not

- **Not a status update.** Telling a steering committee about a risk is
  reporting. Escalating it moves the ownership.
- **Not keeping it too.** An escalated risk leaves this register. If it sits in
  both places it is owned in neither, and both parties assume the other is
  watching.
- **Not a way to avoid deciding.** Escalating everything above Medium is how a
  sponsor stops reading escalations.

Record where it went and who now owns it. That row is closed here with reason
*no longer applicable — escalated to X*, which is a real closure with a real
trail, not a deletion.

## Thresholds

Set them at planning time and enforce them. Two that matter:

- **the response threshold** — above this band, a documented response is required
- **the escalation threshold** — above this band, the project cannot accept the
  residual alone

A threshold routinely crossed without consequence is worse than none: it teaches
everybody the bands are decorative, and then the one that matters is ignored too.

## The audit trail

For every acceptance, and every escalation, record:

| | |
|---|---|
| **who** | a name, not a role and not a committee |
| **when** | a date |
| **what** | the residual as it stood — a rating, not "acceptable" |
| **why** | the basis: what was traded, and what would reverse it |

**Without the last one the trail is useless.** "Accepted by the sponsor on the
14th" tells a post-mortem nothing about whether the decision was reasonable given
what was known. "Accepted because the alternative was a six-week slip and the
customer had confirmed they could absorb one failed batch" can be reviewed.

## Reviewing the trail

At closure, compare the accepted residuals against what actually happened. A
pattern of accepted risks occurring is a finding about the **threshold**, not
about the individual decisions.
