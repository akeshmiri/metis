# 1 · Choose the technique, and say what it will miss

Read `../../references/identification-techniques.md`. Each technique's weakness
is the operative half.

Choose on two questions:

**Who is available?** Delphi needs several experts and several rounds.
Interviews need time. Brainstorming needs a room where the quiet people will
actually speak — if they will not, it produces the loudest person's risk list
and looks like consensus.

**What stage is this?** Early, use the RBS prompt and assumption analysis: there
is little written down to review and few symptoms to trace. Later, document
review and root-cause analysis pay off, and lessons from prior projects become
checkable against what has already occurred here.

## Then say what it will miss

Write down, before starting: *this technique is weak at X, so Y is likely to be
under-represented.* A checklist misses the novel; brainstorming anchors; lessons
over-weight the past. Naming it is what allows a second pass to target the gap
rather than repeating the first pass more loudly.

## Default when there is no strong signal

RBS prompt first — run `risk_categories()` and walk the categories asking what
could go wrong in each (`../../references/risk-breakdown-structure.md` says what
an RBS is for, and why an empty category is the interesting one) — then
assumption analysis on whatever the plan takes for granted. Between them they cover breadth and the certainties nobody questions,
which are the two largest sources of missed risk.
