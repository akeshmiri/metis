# 1 · Choose the threat strategy

## Confirm the polarity first

If the register row is an opportunity, this is the wrong skill — the strategy
sets are disjoint apart from Accept and Escalate. A row carrying "mitigate" on an
opportunity is nearly always mis-polarised rather than badly planned.

## Choose against band and controllability

| Band | Usual choice |
|---|---|
| Very High | **Avoid** if the thing at risk is not essential; otherwise **Mitigate** hard, with a contingency plan |
| High | **Mitigate**; **Transfer** if the impact is purely financial |
| Medium | Mitigate cheaply, or **Accept** actively with a reserve |
| Low | **Accept**, usually passively |

Then override on two questions the table cannot see:

**Is it within the project's control?** If the response needed is beyond the
project manager's remit, it is **Escalate**, whatever the band. An escalated risk
leaves this register — and if it is left sitting here too, nobody owns it.

**Is somebody else better placed to carry it?** **Transfer** when another party
can absorb the financial consequence better. This is a capability question, not a
cost question, and it does not make the event less likely.

## Check the response is worth its cost

A mitigation costing more than the exposure is not a mitigation. For a quantified
risk, compare with `risk_emv`. For a qualitative one, say the comparison is a
judgement and record whose.

## Record the strategy with the reason

The strategy word alone is not a decision. Write why this one and not the obvious
alternative — that sentence is what makes the choice reviewable later, and it is
the first thing missing from registers that turn out to be useless.

## Then plan it

Hand to `metis-risk-manager-response-planning`. A strategy with no owner, trigger
or fallback will not execute, and it looks fine in the register while not doing so.
