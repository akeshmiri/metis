# Risk response strategies

Standard practice, not a Métis decision. Ten strategies, five per polarity.
**Threats and opportunities do not share a strategy set** — `risk_register_check`
reports a crossed pair as `RISK-RESPONSE-POLARITY`, because "mitigate this
opportunity" is almost always a miscategorised risk rather than a real plan.

## Threats — a negative risk

| Strategy | What it does | When it fits | The cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Remove the cause: change scope, approach or technology so the risk cannot occur | High exposure, and the thing at risk is not essential | Usually scope or capability. Avoidance is not free — it is a trade |
| **Mitigate** | Reduce probability, impact, or both, to an acceptable level | The most common choice for High and Very High | Effort spent up front, whether or not the risk occurs |
| **Transfer** | Move the *financial* consequence to a third party — insurance, warranty, a fixed-price contract | The impact is financial and somebody else is better placed to carry it | A premium, and **the risk still occurs** — only who pays changes |
| **Accept** | Take no action beyond recording it. *Active*: set aside a contingency reserve. *Passive*: deal with it if it happens | Low exposure, or a cost of response higher than the exposure | Nothing up front, everything if it occurs |
| **Escalate** | Hand it to whoever owns the authority — it is outside the project's control | The response needed is beyond the project manager's remit | It leaves the register: escalated is not the project's to manage, and pretending otherwise is how it goes unowned |

## Opportunities — a positive risk

| Strategy | What it does | When it fits | The cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exploit** | Make certain it happens — remove the uncertainty | High-value, and within the team's power to guarantee | Committed effort, usually reassigned from something else |
| **Enhance** | Increase probability, impact, or both | The opportunity is real but not certain | Effort with no guaranteed return |
| **Share** | Give part of it to a partner better able to capture it — a joint venture, a partnership | Capturing it needs capability the team does not have | A share of the benefit |
| **Accept** | Take it if it arrives; do nothing to pursue it | Low value, or pursuit costs more than the gain | The opportunity is likely missed |
| **Escalate** | Hand it to whoever can act on it | Beyond the project's scope to pursue | As above — it leaves the register |

## Two mistakes worth naming

**Transfer is not elimination.** A fixed-price contract moves the cost overrun to
a supplier; it does not make the project deliver on time. Registers routinely
close a transferred risk as though the impact were gone, when only the invoice
moved.

**Accept is a decision and must be recorded as one.** An accepted risk with a
named owner and a contingency reserve is risk management. A risk that was never
responded to and drifted into "accepted" is the absence of it, and the two are
indistinguishable in a register six months later unless the reason was written
down.

## The residual and the secondary

Two things a response leaves behind, and both are commonly missed:

- **Residual risk** — what remains *after* the response. Mitigation reduces; it
  rarely eliminates. If the register does not carry the residual, the response
  looks like a closure.
- **Secondary risk** — a *new* risk introduced by the response itself.
  Outsourcing to remove a capacity risk introduces a supplier risk. It belongs in
  the register as its own row.
