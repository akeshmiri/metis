# Risk governance and roles

Standard practice. What an organisation puts around a register so that accepting
a risk is a decision somebody made rather than something that happened.

## The three roles

| Role | Responsible for | Collapsing it causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk owner** | watching the indicator, keeping the row current, calling the trigger | with no single owner, nobody watches, and the risk is discovered when it occurs |
| **Action owner** | executing one response action, by a date | responses owned by "the team" do not happen |
| **Acceptor** | taking the residual on the organisation's behalf | if this is the risk owner, the person with an interest in the risk being acceptable decides that it is |

The risk owner and action owner are often the same person. **The acceptor should
not be**, above the escalation threshold — the separation is the same principle
that stops an author approving their own work.

## Acceptance authority rises with exposure

| Band | Typical acceptor |
|---|---|
| Low | risk owner |
| Medium | project manager |
| High | sponsor |
| Very High | sponsor, recorded outside the project |

The levels are illustrative; the shape is not. Nobody accepts their own risk
above the line, and the line is set before it is needed rather than during the
argument about a specific risk.

## What an acceptance must record

| | Why |
|---|---|
| **who** | a name. Not a role, not a committee |
| **when** | a date |
| **what** | the residual as it stood, as a rating |
| **why** | the basis: what was traded, and what would reverse it |

**The last one carries the weight.** "Accepted by the sponsor on the 14th" tells
a review nothing about whether the decision was reasonable given what was known
then. "Accepted because the alternative was a six-week slip, and the customer had
confirmed they could absorb one failed batch" can be judged — and can be revisited
when the customer changes their mind.

## Escalation

Escalation moves **ownership**, not attention. Two failures:

- **Reporting mistaken for escalating.** Telling a steering committee about a
  risk is reporting. The project still owns it.
- **Escalating and keeping it.** If the row stays in the project register as
  though it were still managed there, both parties assume the other is watching.

An escalated risk closes here with the reason *escalated to X*, which leaves a
trail. It is not a deletion.

## Thresholds

Two, set at planning time:

- the **response threshold** — above this, a documented response is required
- the **escalation threshold** — above this, the project cannot accept alone

A threshold routinely crossed without consequence is worse than having none: it
teaches everybody that the bands are decorative, and the one that matters gets
ignored with them.

## Reviewing the governance, not just the risks

At closure, compare accepted residuals against what happened. A pattern of
accepted risks occurring is a finding about the **threshold**, not about the
individual decisions — and it is the only way the thresholds ever get corrected.
